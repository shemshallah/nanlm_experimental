Kernel: https://www.kaggle.com/code/shem42/r30-ablation-gpu

the geometry-off arm ran after the hybrid on GPU 0.

## 1. MoE utilization on held-out books

128 pairs, top-2 per token, 65,536 validation positions. Two MoE layers.
Union is across both layers (262,144 pair assignments).

| Layer | Active pairs | Effective pairs | Max pair share | Top-8 share | Min / max count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Block 1 | **128 / 128** | 113.5 | 2.16% | 13.49% | 19 / 2,827 |
| Block 3 | **128 / 128** | 107.5 | 2.62% | 16.55% | 4 / 3,430 |
| Union | **128 / 128** | **118.4** | 1.89% | 12.39% | 105 / 4,941 |

Mean count is 2,048 per pair in the union; coefficient of variation 0.394.
20 pairs receive <0.5% of assignments and 3 receive <0.25%, but none are dead.
This is **not** the 18/128 collapse. The earlier 32-pair checkpoint also used
**32/32** pairs (effective 31.6 on a 1,024-position probe).

`pair_bias` was 0.0 in both MoE layers. It is a non-trainable growth gate, so
on a fresh uniform 128-pair model it has nothing to mask. `grow-warmup` only
acts through `--grow-pairs` on an existing smaller checkpoint; it is not
defined for a fresh 128-pair initialization. Using it here would have required
starting at 32 pairs and growing mid-run, breaking the matched-parameter,
matched-step design. Load balance was instead encouraged with the Switch-style
auxiliary loss at weight 0.05, and routing used the batched `gather` dispatch.

## 2. Ablation: hybrid vs plain transformer

| Arm | CE (nats/byte) | BPB | Top-1 next byte |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hybrid (MoE + geometry) | 1.807862 | **2.608194** | 47.00% |
| Plain (no MoE, no geometry) | 1.620171 | **2.337413** | 51.70% |
| Hybrid, geometry off | 1.806342 | **2.606001** | 46.71% |

- **Hybrid − plain = +0.270781 BPB.** The hybrid is worse by 0.27 BPB, not
  better by >0.1. The criterion for “geometry has value” is not met.
- **Hybrid − hybrid_geo0 = +0.002193 BPB.** Geometry contributes essentially
  nothing at this scale; 0.002 BPB is noise-level.
- The training curve shows plain ahead at every 100-step evaluation
  (step 100: 2.255 vs 2.491; step 600: 1.590 vs 1.782 nats/byte), so this is
  not a single-batch fluke.

Important confound: this protocol matches parameters and steps, **not active
compute**. Plain runs all 21.4M parameters densely. The hybrid activates two
of 128 experts per token in two of four blocks, so its active multiply-adds are
roughly 10× lower per token. The negative result therefore says: at this small
scale and token budget, a sparse 21.4M hybrid does not beat a dense 21.4M
transformer given the same number of optimizer steps. It does not prove MoE or
geometry is useless at frontier scale or at matched active FLOPs. The
geometry-only comparison *is* compute-matched, and it is flat.

## 3. Generation from a held-out book prompt

Prompt (held-out book, Wharton, *The Touchstone*):

> It was not that he cared much for the club, but that the remote contingency of having to give it up stood to him, just then, perhaps by

200 generated bytes, sampled (temperature 0.8, top-k 40):

- **Hybrid:** ` the of his broy of herself the all shall leage the dune for the propice along the copaanste_ of my. I cried for you care of for clivence, and stees. OI was it can the boy worked, don alvibeer passerv`
- **Plain:** ` the order of the and a circlingming the cistain of occiate and this than was to somethings ’esin, and all be accame a something; and his handed out towards ago to do he went un no far-ahightfice th`
- **Hybrid, geometry off:** ` the of the dick beegary said they discetion experceails and the pantion of the had have his chars for first for ungastent, the sching a of them, taise he still and the would between evefent. ’s som`

Hybrid greedy (top-k 0) is worse:

> ` the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said the said t`

**None is fluent Gutenberg prose.** The hybrid collapses into repeated
“the said” under greedy decoding. The plain model is slightly more varied,
consistent with its better BPB, but still word salad. The earlier 57.45%
top-1 figure came from only 4,096 positions and did not imply fluent
generation; on 65,536 positions the new hybrid is 47.0% and plain 51.7%.

## What would make the next test decisive

1. **Compute-matched dense baseline.** Compare the 21.4M sparse hybrid against
   a ~3M dense transformer that uses the same active FLOPs per token, not the
   same parameter count.
2. **Grow-warmup experiment.** Start at 32 pairs, train, then grow to 128 with
   `--grow-pairs 128 --grow-warmup N`; log utilization before/after. This is
   the only way to actually exercise `pair_bias`.
3. **More tokens.** 1.23M positions is only ~11% of one epoch. The previous
   6.36M-position run was also not fluent; byte-level fluency needs far more.
4. **Longer training before judging geometry.** The geometry-off delta is
   0.002 BPB at 600 steps; it could grow or vanish with more updates.
